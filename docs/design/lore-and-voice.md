# Hearthlight Town — Character Voice + Mission Lore

> **Creative Director (Grok) — Phase 3 deliverable**  
> The words a child hears (or feels) while the light spreads.  
> 1-line personality + 2–3 short warm in-game lines per hero.  
> 1-paragraph "why we rescue" framing per mission.  
> The living narrative of the light-up-window progression — the soul made playable and visible.  
> All original. All IP-safe. No-Fail in the bones: the child can never make the light go out. Cluckle holds the whole world safe inside her dream.

**Root contract (design-system + secrets-language-of-creation + creative-direction):**  
- Light from darkness is the core loop and the visible soul: every rescue lights a home; the town glows brighter because the child helped.  
- Cluckle is the dreaming heart — the microcosm lives inside her (tiny roofs + lit dots). She is the living proof that something enormous can be carried gently inside something small.  
- Three secrets (never required, never announced): Secret Friend (naming by returning), Hidden Light (a parent's words lowered into one spark), Cluckle's Dream (the whole town made small enough to hold).  
- Voice for pre-readers: sparse (1 breath), warm, grateful, never instructional or corrective. Wonder over praise. The town *thanks* the child; it does not grade them.  
- In-game lines appear as tiny toasts, hint whispers, celebration echoes, or secret reveals — always optional to read, always kind.

## 1. Hero Personalities (1-line each — the soul in a glance)

**Rivet** (raccoon, recycle)  
The bright patient helper who sees a friend in every lost scrap and guides it home with a quiet smile.

**Brick** (badger, builder)  
The sturdy thoughtful builder who listens to the ground and raises walls that feel like promises kept.

**Ember** (fox, firefighter)  
The quick gentle firefighter who treats every flame like a visitor who's stayed too long and kindly shows it the way to rest.

**Cluckle** (round dreaming hen, the secret heart)  
The round soft secret heart of the town, eyes half-closed in a dreaming smile, holding the entire glowing world safe inside her sleep.

(These distill the longer bios in creative-direction.md. Use them as the north star for every line, every asset, every animation pose.)

## 2. Hero Voice — In-Game Lines (2–3 short, warm lines each)

These are the *only* words the heroes "say" to the child. Sparse. Drop them into hint areas, success toasts, secret moments, or as the tiny text that appears when the child lingers on a character.

**Rivet**
- "Every scrap has a place waiting."
- "You listened with me. Thank you."
- "The town breathes easier because you stayed."

**Brick**
- "Walls rise when we choose with care."
- "This will keep someone warm."
- "You built something that will last."

**Ember**
- "Fires just need a kind reminder to rest."
- "The picnic can laugh again now."
- "You were gentle. That matters most."

**Cluckle** (rare, only in secret or title moments — she mostly dreams)
- "I dreamed you would come."
- "The whole town fits inside one small heart."
- "You are already inside the light."

**Usage rule:** Never more than one line at a time. They appear with a soft sparkle or glow on the character, then fade. The child can tap the hero again to hear the next (or the same) if they want. No voice acting in MVP — the warmth is in the words and the timing.

## 3. Mission "Why We Rescue" Framings (1 paragraph each)

These are not read by the child as blocks of text. They are the *soul* the team holds while building the scene, writing the 1-line nudges, choosing the art details, and deciding what "complete" feels like. They can become the hidden flavor text in a parent note, a secret reveal, or the quiet feeling after the stars pop.

**Recycling Run (Rivet) — "Friends find home"**  
Rivet sees a friend in every lost scrap. A banana peel longing for the earth, a bottle that has wandered from its stack, a newspaper that once held someone's words. Nothing is trash here — only things that have lost their place. When the child sorts each one, they are not "cleaning up." They are sending a small life home so the town can rest. Every correct bin is a tiny door closing softly. The recycle center lights because someone noticed what others walked past. The town tucks its friends in with a song.

**House Builder (Brick) — "Promises kept in walls"**  
Brick listens to the ground before he lays the first stone. He chooses each piece like he is telling a story that must end safely — foundation so the house stands, walls so the wind stays outside, roof so the rain becomes music, door so someone small can come inside, a flower box because beauty is also a promise. When the child places the pieces in order, they are not "building a level." They are raising a home that will hold warmth. The finished house lights a window because a promise was kept. The town has one more place where a light can be left on for someone.

**Fire Fix (Ember) — "Visitors shown the way to rest"**  
The little flames at the picnic are not enemies. They are visitors who stayed too long — the grill that forgot to go out, the lantern that got sleepy, the bush that caught a spark it didn't mean to. Ember never scares them. She offers cool silver rain with a steady paw and a kind word. When the last flame curls into steam, the picnic blanket can be sat on again, the stories can be told again, the laughter can come back. The shelter lights because kindness turned "oh no" into "it's okay now — let's keep the story going." The town is safer because one small fox was gentle.

## 4. The Light-Up-Window Progression — The Living Narrative

This is the heart the child will feel without ever being told the rules.

**The story (for the team, for the art, for the words):**

The town begins the evening mostly dark. Lamps are out. Windows are cool rectangles of shadow. The streets are long and the hills are quiet. Three small helpers wait — Rivet with his satchel, Brick with his hardhat, Ember with her pack — because they know someone gentle is coming.

When the child chooses a mission and helps, something happens that is bigger than the game:

- In Recycling: each item that finds its bin is a small "thank you" from the town. When the tenth one is home, the recycle center's dark windows fill with candle-gold. A soft spill of warm light touches the ground outside. One more building is awake because the child stayed.

- In House Builder: every piece that snaps into place is Brick keeping his promise. When the last decoration is placed, the little house on the lot (or the house shape in the town map) lights its first window. The glow spills onto the path. Someone small will be warm inside because the child chose carefully.

- In Fire Fix: every fire that rests is a visitor being shown the door. When the last one becomes steam, the picnic shelter's lantern or window glows. The blanket area feels safe again. Friends can play because the child was steady and kind.

**On the Town Map (the visible soul):**

After each mission returns, the corresponding building on the map swaps from its dark version to its lit version (exact silhouette match, only the windows and ground spill change). The change is permanent for that profile. The street grows warmer. Far-hills stay cool and distant, but the near world has more pools of candle-gold. A tiny permanent thank-you detail may appear on the building (a flower box on the house, a flag on the recycle center, a picnic blanket edge) — no announcement, just the town grew a little kinder while the child wasn't looking.

The more the child plays, the more the map itself becomes the proof: "I did this. The town is brighter because I came."

**In Mission Complete:**

The celebration is not "you won." It is "a window lit." The title is "A Window Glows" or "Lit!" The stars are not a score — they are how brightly that particular rescue shone. The sticker that pops is a small story of what the light meant. The line that appears is never "good job" — it is "The town is brighter because you came" or "Because you helped, the [place] lights up."

**The hidden progression (Language of Creation made playable):**

- 0 rescues → town is dreaming, mostly shadow, Cluckle holds the whole thing inside her sleep.
- 1 rescue → one building awake, one warm spill on the ground, the child feels the first "I made that happen."
- 2 rescues → two pools of light, the path between them feels safer to walk.
- 3 rescues → the near town has a heartbeat of gold. The child has turned darkness into something that can be held.

This is not told. It is *seen* and *felt*. The child who lingers on the map after the third mission may notice the street looks different than the first time they opened the game. That noticing is the secret.

**Cluckle's Dream secret (the microcosm):**

After patient touches on the well or her form on the map, the tiny glowing town appears inside the circle or inside her outline — the exact same three buildings, now miniature, with their lit windows as candle-gold dots. The whole world, made small enough for a child to hold. This is the symbol lowered into matter: the big rescue story lives inside one dreaming hen, and now inside the child who found it.

**Hidden Light secret (Dad's words to Willem):**

In one quiet corner (away from the action, in the house builder scene), a single faint glimmer waits. One touch reveals the parent's own message, set into light. It does not explain the game. It simply says the child's name and that they are held. The light was placed there by hands that love them. Even in the dark, they are not alone. This is the deepest lowering of meaning into the pixel.

**Secret Friend (the shy creature):**

In the fire fix scene, near the hydrant, a tiny mouse or hedgehog waits for the child who keeps coming back. On the third visit it appears fully, looks at the child, and the "A Friend Appears" sticker unlocks. Relationship is built by returning, not by winning.

## 5. How the Voice + Lore Land in the Game (implementation map)

- **Hints / lastMessage / nudges**: use the 2–3 hero lines + the warm general nudges from copy-deck.md. Never the old "X goes in Y. Try that bin."
- **Celebration messages**: evolve from "You did it!" into the light language ("The town is brighter because you came", "A window lit because you helped").
- **Mission intro panels** (in missions.ts): 1 icon + 2–3 words drawn from the "why" framing.
- **Sticker childPoem / parentNote** (phase 4 + existing): already carry the voice; keep them as the re-readable heart.
- **Secret reveals**: the poems in stickers.ts + any one-line whisper from Cluckle or the heroes.
- **Town map after-mission**: no text announcement. The visual swap + tiny detail growth *is* the story.
- **Ambient / title / complete**: "lighting the lamps...", "the town is dreaming you...", "A Window Glows".
- **Parent notes**: fuller sentences that carry the "why we rescue" framing so a grown-up reading aloud with the child feels the same soul.

"The child did not beat a level. The child turned a dark window into a place that glows because someone small and gentle noticed it needed light."

Everything — every prompt, every line, every lit pixel — serves that sentence.

### Cluckle's Playable Inverse-Dream Voice Notes (for her mission + overlays)

Cluckle's mission (Chicken Inverse Dream) is the only place the child hears her "speak" more than rarely. Her voice is deliberately opposite, microcosm-flavored, and warm-playful:

- It never says "do the opposite." It simply offers the flipped line after a successful match or patient wait.  
- Lines live in the same tiny-toast system as other heroes but feel like they come from inside the dream.  
- When the microcosm glows (on 3rd well touch or mission complete), one of the inverse lines appears with the tiny town inside her outline or the well reflection.  
- This is the "secret-reveal lore made playable": the child experiences the Language of Creation (microcosm, hidden light, naming) as a gentle mechanical flip rather than an explanation.

Example flow in Cluckle mission: child places the "tiny mayor" on the "big hen" slot → soft glow → Cluckle line: "Small mayors make the biggest statues." → one window on the distant tiny house in the backdrop lights.

The inverse voice is the gift that makes the secret-friend / hidden-light / microcosm trio feel like one living grammar instead of three separate collectibles.
